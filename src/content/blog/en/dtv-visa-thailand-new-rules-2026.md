**In brief:** Since 31 August 2026, the DTV visa for Thailand has stricter rules: you must apply at the embassy in your country of nationality or permanent residence, and provide proof of residence plus a police clearance certificate. Prepare a strong application to maximise your chances.

*Information verified in November 2026. Rules may change: always check with the relevant embassy before applying.*

## What are the new DTV visa rules from August 2026?

Since 31 August 2026, several major changes affect the DTV (Destination Thailand Visa) application process. The main updates are:

- **Mandatory application at the embassy in your country of nationality or permanent residence:** For example, a French citizen living abroad must apply via the embassy in their home or resident country—not while travelling elsewhere.
- **Proof of permanent residence required:** Such as an official residency certificate, residence card, etc.
- **Police clearance certificate:** (For France, a recent bulletin n°3) is now compulsory.
- **Thai language schools excluded** from the Soft Power category (apply for an ED visa for these courses).
- **Bank funds scrutiny:** Sudden large deposits (“fund parking”) are a common reason for refusal.

These are in addition to the standard DTV visa conditions: multiple-entry visa valid for 5 years, 180 days per entry (renewable once), and a strict ban on working for Thai companies or clients.

## Who can apply for the DTV visa under the new rules?

The DTV visa remains open to several profiles, but each category has specific requirements:

- **Workcation:** Employees working remotely for a foreign company, freelancers, or self-employed with non-Thai clients. Evidence: employment contract or employer letter, or contracts/invoices for at least 6 months.
- **Soft Power:** Enrolment in a recognised programme (Muay Thai, Thai cooking, traditional medicine, arts, sports, seminars). Evidence: detailed acceptance letter from a registered institution.
- **Medical:** Treatment plan and quote from an approved hospital or clinic.
- **Dependent:** Legal spouse and children under 20 of a DTV holder.

> **Note:** Parents, siblings, or unmarried partners are not eligible as dependents.

## What are the main reasons for DTV visa refusal under the new rules?

Experience shows refusals are often due to:

- **Insufficient or unstable bank funds:** The balance must be positive each month for the last 3 months, with no sudden large deposits.
- **Missing proof of residence or police certificate.**
- **Application submitted from Thailand or the wrong embassy.**
- **Weak professional evidence:** LinkedIn profile alone, no contracts or invoices for freelancers.
- **Non-recognised Soft Power programme:** Thai language schools are excluded.

## DTV visa new rules: advice from an expat in Koh Samui

Having lived in Thailand for several years on a DTV visa, I’ve seen the process become much more rigorous since August 2026. Authorities now closely examine the stability of your funds and the consistency of your documents. It’s not enough to have a large sum in your account: regularity matters as much as the amount.

Another key point: the Paris embassy is especially strict about proof of residence and the validity of police certificates. Prepare these documents in advance and check their acceptability with your embassy.

Finally, the Soft Power category is now limited to very specific programmes: forget about Thai language schools, which now fall under the ED visa.

Careful preparation, anticipating translations/legalisations, and ensuring every document matches your real situation are the keys to avoiding nasty surprises.
